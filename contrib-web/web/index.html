An open corpus, given one chat at a time

Your chats can teach an open model.

The conversations people have with ChatGPT and Claude show what real work with an assistant looks like: what people ask, how the model goes about it, and whether it helped. Almost all of that sits inside closed labs. Share one of yours, and it becomes part of an openly licensed dataset anyone can use to train open-weight assistants.

Why share a conversation?

Cedar is an open-source assistant that runs on hardware you own. Assistants like it depend on open-weight models, and open models learn to do agent work, like searching, running code and using tools, from examples of exactly that.

01

Open models need real tasks

Benchmarks are made up. Your conversation is a real request, with the model's real attempt at it: the tool calls, the code, the dead ends and the answer.

02

Your verdict is the signal

A transcript alone teaches little. Whether it worked, partly worked or failed, and your thumbs on single answers, are what reinforcement learning trains on.

03

Given, never taken

Nothing is collected in the background. Every conversation in the corpus is one a person chose, read as it would be sent, and could take back.

What happens after you press Send

  1. ReceivedThe corpus checks the format and looks again for secrets. Anything with a leftover key is refused, not stored.
  2. ScreenedAutomated checks look for duplicates, hidden instructions, harmful commands and personal data the redaction missed. Doubtful ones go to a person.
  3. HeldAccepted conversations wait at least two weeks, so you can still withdraw before anything is published.
  4. ReleasedA release, about once a month, publishes the conversations as an open dataset under the license each person chose, signed and with a list of withdrawn items that copies must honour.

How to share your chats

It takes a few minutes, and the slowest part is waiting for the export email.

  1. 1

    Export your data

    1. In ChatGPT, open Settings → Data controls.
    2. Choose Export data and confirm.
    3. OpenAI emails you a link, usually within minutes. Download the .zip.
    1. On claude.ai, open Settings → Privacy.
    2. Choose Export data.
    3. Anthropic emails you a link. Download the .zip.
  2. 2

    Drop the .zip below

    No need to unpack it. This page reads it on your device and lists your conversations by title. Nothing is uploaded, and the titles are never sent.

  3. 3

    Pick one and read it

    You see the conversation exactly as it would be sent, with every replaced value highlighted. Select any name, place or employer to replace it too, and say how it went.

  4. 4

    Send, and keep the receipt

    Choose a license and, if you like, a random pseudonym. Your receipt stays in this browser, and its Withdraw button takes the conversation back out.

What leaves your browser, and what never does

Sent, after you've read it

  • Your prompts and the model's answers, in the one conversation you picked
  • The model's tool calls and their results, such as code it ran
  • Which model answered, and the day of each message
  • Your outcome, ratings and note, plus the license you chose
  • Fake stand-ins where keys, emails, numbers, addresses and user names were

Never sent

  • The rest of your export: every other conversation stays on your device
  • Conversation titles, account details, ids and times of day
  • Files and images you uploaded (only their type and size)
  • ChatGPT's custom instructions and memories, and Claude's memory
  • Web pages the model read, unless you choose to include them
  • Anything from connectors to your mail, calendar or drives. Those chats can't be shared at all.

Be honest with yourself about the rest. The automatic redaction finds things with a known shape. It can't know that "the oat-milk startup in Portland" is you. Read before you send, and replace what identifies you or other people.

Questions

Is my export uploaded anywhere?

No. The page reads the .zip with your browser and builds the contribution on your device. The only request that carries conversation text is the one you start with Send, and it carries only the conversation you reviewed. You can check that in your browser's network panel.

Can I take a contribution back?

Yes. Each receipt has a Withdraw button. A withdrawn conversation is deleted from the queue and left out of every later release. A release that's already published can't be recalled from people who downloaded it, which is why accepted conversations wait at least two weeks first.

Do I need an account? Will my name be attached?

No account. By default each contribution stands alone and can't be linked to your others. If you want credit or want your contributions grouped, you can use a random pseudonym that this browser generates. It's never derived from who you are.

Which license should I pick?

CC0 places your part in the public domain and is the most useful for training. CC BY 4.0 asks anyone using it to credit your pseudonym. Either way, you're licensing what you wrote: your prompts and your feedback.

Are ChatGPT and Claude answers allowed in the dataset?

OpenAI's and Anthropic's terms restrict using their models' outputs to train other models. The corpus publishes a model's outputs only where the people running it have decided that's allowed. If it doesn't accept a model yet, this page tells you during review and Send stays off, before anything leaves your browser.

Why can't I share some conversations?

Conversations that used connectors to your mail, calendar, drives or work apps contain other people's data, so they're blocked. Conversations where a secret is still visible, or that are too large, are blocked until you fix them. Sharing only the first few exchanges usually helps.

Who runs this?

The Cedar project, which is open source under Apache 2.0. The service that receives contributions keeps no request logs and no IP addresses. Its code, the screening rules and the release process are all in the repository.
